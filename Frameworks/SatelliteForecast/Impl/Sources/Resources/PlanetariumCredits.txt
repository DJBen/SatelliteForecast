Planetarium rendering

Star billboard optics, magnitude/exposure model, spectral colors and wavelengths
adapted from DJBen/StarryNight, Planetarium/Metal/StarRenderer.swift and
Planetarium/Metal/StarShaders.metal. Camera layering and tiered rendering inspired
by the same project. https://github.com/DJBen/StarryNight/tree/master/Planetarium

Landscape: original procedural Metal mountains and lake. The low, periodic
ridge is illustrative, not terrain for the observer's actual location.
View-dependent Fresnel reflection, filtered directional gravity waves, and a shallow bed
replace the former ground panorama; no landscape bitmap is used.
Bright-star diffraction accents are artistic camera optics, not stellar structure.
Optical reference: https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/webbs-diffraction-spikes/

Planetarium Milky Way: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization
Studio; Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC. https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/
Source: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004800/a004851/milkyway_2020_16k_gal.exr
SHA-256: ee023f03e99466b3829117c15a001269ba32a3fecb6012d0b2d41f201c955351
Galactic coordinates, corrected January 2021; no separate bright-star layer.
Native 16384 × 8192, linear-to-sRGB conversion, no blur or upscaling.
Two 8192 × 8192 ASTC 6×6 sRGB tiles, each with 14 linear-light mip levels.
KTX 1 containers keep native 16K detail within an 8K GPU texture limit;
approximately 76 MiB total compressed storage. Tile boundaries wrap in shader.
The low-resolution chart texture is intentionally separate and unchanged.
Reproduction: scripts/prepare-planetarium-textures.py (ARM astcenc 5.7.0).

Lunar albedo: existing NASA resource; see bundled lunar credits.

Water rendering research references (original shader implementation):
GPU Gems, Chapter 1: Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models.
https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models
Bruneton, Neyret, Holzschuch: Real-time Realistic Ocean Lighting (2010).
https://morpho.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2010/BNH10/

Selection-card planet maps: Solar System Scope / INOVE, based on NASA imagery.
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/
CC BY 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Downloaded 2K maps and resized to 512 x 256 for small Metal globes:
Mercury, Venus Atmosphere, Earth Day Map and Clouds, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn,
Uranus and Neptune. Source filenames: 2k_<name>.jpg in the texture download
folder; bundled derivatives use the planet-icon- prefix. Planet colors and
unmapped areas are artistic approximations as documented by the author.
Rotation periods: NASA Planetary Fact Sheet (hours, signed for retrograde).
https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/
Earth's thumbnail uses the requested exact 24-hour / 10-second normalization.
Venus displays opaque sulfuric-acid clouds; its illustrative cloud texture
rotates with the body, not a separate atmospheric superrotation simulation.

Detailed Moon map: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit (2025 map),
LROC WAC mosaic, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University; visualization Ernie Wright.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_16bit_srgb_4k.tif
Converted to 4096x2048 JPEG at quality 95 for the planetarium, without upscaling.

Major planetary moons: positions from NASA/JPL Horizons (ICRF, geocentric,
light-time corrected vectors); radii and sampling periods from JPL SSD satellite
physical parameters/mean elements. https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html
https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/  https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/
128px moon materials are original procedural illustrations, not NASA surface maps.

Saturn globe/ring dimensions: NASA NSSDCA Saturn and Saturnian Rings Fact Sheets
https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/saturnfact.html
https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html
Planet phase validation: NASA/JPL Horizons observer ephemerides.
